\hourhead{16}{TEACH}{Embeddings and vector search with FAISS}{SST-2 sentences (8,741) as a search corpus}{Embeddings, vector databases, ANN indexing (IVF, HNSW)}

An \textbf{embedding} maps text to a vector so that similar meanings land close together. With unit-length vectors, cosine similarity is just a dot product: $\cos(u,v)=u^\top v$.

We build 256-dimensional embeddings with \textbf{LSA} (TF-IDF followed by truncated SVD), which needs no download; in class, swap in a sentence-transformer such as \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}. The FAISS code does not change.

\begin{lstlisting}
flat = faiss.IndexFlatIP(d); flat.add(E)                      # exact
ivf  = faiss.IndexIVFFlat(faiss.IndexFlatIP(d), d, 64, faiss.METRIC_INNER_PRODUCT)
ivf.train(E); ivf.add(E); ivf.nprobe = 6                       # search 6 of 64 clusters
hnsw = faiss.IndexHNSWFlat(d, 32, faiss.METRIC_INNER_PRODUCT); hnsw.add(E)   # graph
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{resultbox}[500 queries, top-10 neighbours (timings depend on the machine and its load)]
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Index & Recall@10 vs exact & $\mu$s per query\\
\midrule
Flat (exact) & 1.000 & \tFlat\\
IVF (64 lists, nprobe 6) & \rIVF & \tIVF\\
HNSW ($M=32$) & \rHNSW & \tHNSW\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[3pt]
Query ``a heartwarming film for the whole family'' returns ``a film that will enthrall the whole family''.
\end{resultbox}

\begin{keyidea}[Approximate search is a dial]
IVF scans only a few clusters; HNSW walks a navigable graph. Both trade a little recall for large speed-ups --- essential at millions of vectors. Chroma and pgvector add storage, metadata filters and SQL on top of the same ideas.
\end{keyidea}

\hourhead{17}{TEACH}{Hybrid retrieval, re-ranking and retrieval metrics}{TruthfulQA (790 questions + look-alike false answers)}{BM25 + dense hybrid, re-rankers, Recall@k, MRR, nDCG}

\textbf{The task.} For each question, find its \emph{true} best answer in a corpus that also contains every question's best \emph{incorrect} answer --- a hard negative that often uses the same words.

\begin{defbox}[BM25, fusion and metrics]
\textbf{BM25} scores a document by term frequency (with saturation) and rarity (IDF), normalised by length.
\textbf{Reciprocal-rank fusion:} $\mathrm{RRF}(d)=\sum_{r}\frac{1}{k+\mathrm{rank}_r(d)}$ with $k=60$.
\textbf{Recall@k:} share of questions whose gold answer is in the top $k$. \textbf{MRR:} mean of $1/\mathrm{rank}$.
\textbf{nDCG@10:} $\frac{1}{\log_2(\mathrm{rank}+1)}$ if rank $\le 10$, else 0.
\end{defbox}

\begin{resultbox}[All 790 questions]
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
Retriever & R@1 & R@5 & MRR & nDCG@10 & Misconception@1\\
\midrule
BM25 & 0.377 & 0.772 & 0.567 & 0.625 & 0.471\\
Dense (LSA) & 0.384 & 0.737 & 0.547 & 0.600 & 0.381\\
Hybrid (RRF) & \textbf{0.462} & \textbf{0.772} & \textbf{0.609} & \textbf{0.657} & \textbf{0.362}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[3pt]
Learned re-ranker on the held-out half: hybrid R@1 0.468 $\rightarrow$ with re-ranker 0.423 (worse).
\end{resultbox}
\figw[0.7]{s4_retrieval.png}{Recall and misconception rate. Hybrid fusion helps; hand-made re-ranking features do not.}

\begin{keyidea}[Retrieval finds matches, not truth]
BM25 puts the question's own \emph{false} answer first 47\% of the time. A RAG system inherits whatever its corpus says, so curate the corpus. And always test a re-ranker on held-out questions: our hand-made features cannot tell a true statement from a fluent false one; a cross-encoder that reads query and passage together is the real fix.
\end{keyidea}

\hourhead{18}{BUILD}{PolicyGPT: ingest and chunk a real PDF}{Course syllabus PDF (20 pages; NEP 2020 / UGC PDFs in class)}{PDF parsing, chunking strategy, metadata}

\begin{lstlisting}
reader = PdfReader("../../syllabus/AlgoProfessor_AI_Scientist_Syllabus.pdf")
pages = [(i + 1, clean(p.extract_text())) for i, p in enumerate(reader.pages)]
chunks = sliding_windows(pages, size=90, overlap=30)     # every chunk keeps its page number
\end{lstlisting}
\begin{resultbox}
20 pages, 4,761 words $\rightarrow$ 79 chunks of about 90 words, each tagged with its page for citations.
\end{resultbox}

\textbf{Chunking trade-off:} small chunks give precise citations but lose context; large chunks keep context but dilute relevance. Overlap stops facts being cut in half at a boundary.

\hourhead{19}{BUILD}{Cited Q\&A API with abstention}{Course syllabus PDF}{Hybrid search, grounded citations, abstention, FastAPI}

\textbf{Pipeline:} hybrid retrieval of the top 3 chunks $\rightarrow$ coverage check $\rightarrow$ extract the best 25-word span $\rightarrow$ return the answer with page citations.

\begin{defbox}[Coverage-based abstention]
$\mathrm{coverage}(q)=\dfrac{\sum_{w\in q,\;w\in\text{chunks}}\mathrm{idf}(w)}{\sum_{w\in q}\mathrm{idf}(w)}$. Words the documents never use get the highest IDF. If coverage is below a threshold, answer ``I can't find this in the documents.''
\end{defbox}

\begin{resultbox}
``What is the programme fee in India?'' $\rightarrow$ ``\ldots Programme fee INR 50,000 (Hyderabad \& Shirdi, India)'' [p.\,1].\\
``What is the hostel fee?'' $\rightarrow$ \emph{abstains} (coverage 0.40): ``fee'' matches, ``hostel'' appears nowhere.
\end{resultbox}

def ask(req: Ask): return {"question": req.question, **answer(req.question, req.k)}

\hourhead{20}{BUILD}{Evaluate: dev set to tune, golden set to test}{8-question dev set + 25-question golden set}{RAG evaluation, hallucination measurement, regression testing}

\textbf{Method.} Choose the abstention threshold on a separate \emph{dev} set (5 answerable + 3 unanswerable). Then report once on the untouched \emph{golden} set (20 answerable + 5 unanswerable).

\begin{resultbox}[Golden set, threshold 0.50 chosen on the dev set]
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Retrieval hit@3 (answerable) & 0.95\\
Answer contains the gold fact (answerable) & 0.60\\
Wrongly abstained (answerable) & 0.00\\
Correctly abstained (unanswerable) & 1.00\\
Faithfulness (answer words found in cited chunks) & 1.00\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{resultbox}

\begin{pitfall}[How we got here]
Version 1 used a hand-picked threshold and wrongly abstained on 55\% of answerable questions, because question words like ``earns'' and ``covers'' never appear in the documents. Ignoring question-phrasing words and tuning on a dev set fixed it. Never tune on the set you report.
\end{pitfall}

\textbf{Failure analysis.} Where retrieval hit@3 is true but the answer is wrong, the right page was found and the simple span-picker chose the wrong 25 words. An LLM generator does that step better --- with the same citations and the same abstention rule. Keep the golden set in \texttt{tests/} and re-run it on every change; grow it to 100 questions as a team. In class, add \texttt{ragas} faithfulness and answer-relevance scores.
